## 370.3 Filing a claim: what counts

A carrier may voluntarily pay a claim for loss, damage, injury or delay to cargo only when the claim is filed with the proper carrier within the time limits in the bill of lading or contract and the applicable tariff.

A written claim must do three things to meet the minimum filing requirements of 370.3(b): identify the shipment, assert that the carrier is liable for the loss, damage, injury or delay, and make a claim for payment of a specified or determinable amount of money. The rule adds one proviso: procedures must be in place so that the carrier has reasonable access to the documents that support the claim.

## 370.3(c) Documents that are not claims

Bad order reports, appraisal reports of damage, notations of shortage or damage on freight bills or delivery receipts, and inspection reports do not by themselves meet the minimum filing requirements. A notation of damage on a delivery receipt is evidence, not a filed claim.

## 370.5 Acknowledging a claim

A carrier that receives a proper written claim must acknowledge it in writing to the claimant within 30 days after receiving it, unless the carrier has already paid or declined the claim in writing within those 30 days. The acknowledgment tells the claimant what additional documents or information the carrier needs to process the claim.

## 370.5(b) Claim file and claim number

On receiving a claim, the carrier opens a separate file and gives it a claim file number that appears on every related document and letter. The date the claim was received is recorded on the claim and repeated in the acknowledgment.

## 370.7 Investigating a claim

Each claim must be promptly and thoroughly investigated, unless it was already investigated before the claim arrived.

When they are a necessary part of the investigation, a claim is supported by the bill of lading, evidence of any freight charges, and the invoice (or a copy or extract the claimant certifies as true and correct for the goods and value claimed), or a certification of prices or values with any discounts, allowances, deductions or depreciation. Where the goods were never invoiced to the consignee, the invoice shows no price or value, the goods were sold, or they moved at bookkeeping values only, the carrier must have the claimant establish the destination value of the quantity involved before it voluntarily pays. Supporting documents that were a necessary part of an investigation are kept by the carrier for possible FMCSA inspection.

## 370.7(c) Verifying the loss of a whole package or shipment

When a claim for the loss of an entire package or an entire shipment cannot be authenticated any other way, the carrier must obtain a certified statement in writing from the consignee that the property has not been received from any other source.

## 370.9 Disposing of a claim: the 120-day rule

Within 120 days after receiving a written claim, the carrier must pay it, decline it, or make a firm compromise settlement offer, in writing. If the carrier cannot dispose of the claim within 120 days, it must write to the claimant at that point, and every 60 days after while the claim is pending, giving the status of the claim and the reason for the delay, and keep a copy of each of those letters in the claim file.
